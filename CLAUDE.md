# COTEXT

A Bible study app for students learning Biblical Greek and Hebrew.

## The plan (2026-11-01; first build 2026-11-02 - see Done below)

- **Core:** an interlinear (original text with English beneath each word) and parsing information for every word - each switched on and off by the reader.
- **Parsing as shown:** compact, readable without tapping, no spaces - all caps, Stem-Form-PGN with hyphens, e.g. Q-P-3MS (Qal perfect 3ms), QP-P-3MS (Qal passive), HI-W-3MS (Hiphil wayyiqtol), N-MP, a prefix joined with + (ART+N-MP, CONJ+OBJ); verbs colored by form in Hebrew (wayyiqtol, qatal, weqatal, yiqtol, volitives, participles, infinitives) and by tense in Greek (VERB COLORS in the ⋯ menu turns it off; ABOUT VERB COLORS beside it says what each color means). Converted once, when the database is built, from the source's codes.
- **A word tapped:** its concise meaning (built - see Done), its lexicon entry (STEPBible's brief lexicons, TBESH / TBESG - the same extended Strong's keys as the text) - or, as a setting, the fuller entry (BDB for Hebrew, Abbott-Smith likely for Greek; public domain) - and its parsing spelled out (Qal perfect, 3rd masculine singular).
- **Helps controls** (behind a HELPS button): a frequency slider - glosses hidden for words occurring N+ times, parsings for forms occurring N+ times, showing how many words (by frequency) you'd need to know to read them; frequency counted over the whole OT (or NT) or this book alone (book-specific vocab); HIDE KNOWN WORDS (on / off). A word's panel marks that word known by hand ("+ Known words", its gloss hidden), every form of it, on top of the slider.
- **Grammar from the word's panel** (built for Gesenius and Smyth - see Done): each part of the spelled-out parsing tappable (Qal, perfect, 3ms...) - opens that part's explanation, under the word's info, from a grammar picked in a setting (several to try, then one chosen): Hebrew - Gesenius (Kautzsch-Cowley 1910, public domain); Greek - Smyth (1920; Perseus CC BY-SA, classical), Robertson (1914, the fullest NT grammar; scans only - topics mapped to pages, text built section by section), Ewald's Elementary New Testament Greek (2022, digital text, CC BY-NC-SA - non-commercial only), Moulton, Nunn, Goodwin; linked by section or page.
- **Verb stems highlighted:** pick stems (Hebrew binyanim - Qal, Niphal, Piel...; Greek tense / voice / mood) and see them marked through the text.
- **Vocab:** flashcards for any range (a book, a chapter, any span) and by frequency.
- **Verb paradigm flashcards:** a form's pattern - the root's letters as empty dotted squares with their vowels, the form's own letters (prefixes, endings) written - and, turned over, its parsing with its meaning on "do": Q-P-3MS, "he did" (and the pattern on a model root).
- **Spoken answers:** on a vocab flashcard, say the English meaning instead of tapping - the browser's own speech recognition (Safari on iPhone / iPad included; audio goes to Apple), matched leniently against the word's glosses and lexicon senses ("create" / "created" / "to create"), showing what it heard; the reader can still mark it right or wrong.
- **Audio:** modern pronunciation for both (modern Greek, modern Israeli Hebrew - the user's choice, and what they teach): the device's built-in voices read words and verses, and reading aloud can be recognised and followed word by word.
- **Vocab:** each person's words to learn and known, as lists and flashcards (see Done).
- **Reader's version:** a selective interlinear - glosses shown only for words rarer than a frequency the reader sets; a button shows everything when unsure. A way to quiz yourself on reading.
- **Loftier goal:** Bible translation tools - translating from the interlinear into a new version.
- **Also:** generate PDFs (a print stylesheet first; Typst on the server for book-quality typesetting).

## Done (first build, 2026-11-02)

- **Data:** STEPBible-Data (TAHOT, TAGNT, TBESH, TBESG) built into one SQLite database - words, COTEXT parsing codes and their spelled-out parsing, lexicon, per-lemma frequencies (whole OT / NT and per book). MACULA's syntax trees make the sentence diagrams (notes panel).
- **Reader:** pick a book and chapter (‹ › step through); the interlinear as in the mock-ups - original, gloss, parsing code, verbs colored by form (Hebrew) or tense (Greek); a word tapped opens the panel: dictionary form, parsing spelled out, brief lexicon entry.
- **App:** a PWA, offline after the first load (the database is kept by the service worker).
- **Helps menu (⋯):** an overlay under the heading (the words never move); HIDE KNOWN WORDS, FREQUENCY (whole OT / NT or the book shown), the frequency slider - in steps of 20 words up to 1,000 (as Vocab's lists), with the percentage of the scope's text they cover: it hides the gloss and the parsing of the N most common words by the root word (freq), as the printed reader's editions do - SHOW MY VOCAB (on at first) keeping every word in My vocab helped whatever the slider says - but a verb's form hard to recognize (a weak root's - I-nun, I-yod, hollow, III-he, III-alef, geminate; a second aorist, perfect, -μι verb, liquid or suppletive future / aorist: paradigm.ts' hardForm) always keeps its parsing and gloss (the gloss unless known by hand); "+ Known words" in a word's panel (the gloss hidden everywhere; ✓ In known words takes it off). Kept on the device (localStorage).
- **Study menu (☰):** the drawer - Vocab, Verb paradigms, Verb chart, Parsing guide, Pronunciation (Greek: the modern Greek alphabet and letter pairs; Hebrew: the letters and vowels as Israeli Hebrew says them - each with a speaker), each a full screen for the testament shown, its ✕ back to the text; a screen not built says "Coming soon".
- **Verb chart:** Hebrew - a stem (Qal, Niphal, Piel, Pual, Hiphil, Hophal, Hithpael), a row per person / gender / number, columns perfect, imperfect, wayyiqtol, imperative, then participles and infinitives; Greek - a tense and voice (present, imperfect, future, aorist, perfect; active, middle, passive), indicative / subjunctive / imperative by person and number, then infinitive and participle. Each cell its pattern (dotted squares, the form's own letters), COTEXT code and meaning on "do". The verb: Pattern, a model verb (קטל, שׁמר, משׁל, כתב; λύω, πιστεύω, παιδεύω - hand-written templates from Gesenius' Paradigm B and Smyth's λύω, checked against the text's forms of the same verbs), a verb of the chapter or a word's own (its panel's Verb chart) - its actual forms in the text (the commonest spelling per code, with its count; the stem picker counting each stem).
- **Verb paradigms:** flashcards, a deck per stem (Hebrew) or tense and voice (Greek) from the chart's templates: the front a form's pattern, the back its code, meaning on "do" and the form on קָטַל / λύω; BACK / NEXT, SHUFFLE, START OVER, REMOVE FROM LIST.
- **Parsing guide:** every abbreviation of the codes for the text shown's language - Hebrew (and Aramaic) for the OT, Greek for the NT - grouped (how a code reads, parts of speech, stems, forms, person / gender / number...; tense, voice, mood, case...), each with its meaning: the guide table, made from morph.py's own tables.
- **Vocab:** "+ My vocab" in a word's panel; known = "+ Known words" (the helps' hand choice: its gloss hidden). The screen (☰ > Vocab), per testament: four lists - OT / NT FREQUENCY and BOOK FREQUENCY (a book picked, the one shown at first) (the words not yet known, most frequent first in the testament or that book, picked 20 at a time), MY VOCAB and KNOWN WORDS - the one picked (OT / NT frequency at first) as flashcards (front the word with a speaker, back only its concise meaning; ENGLISH TO HEBREW / GREEK the other way round; both sides drawn alike), then, with SHOW LIST (off at first, for quizzing), its words (word, concise meaning, count). A card's REMOVE FROM LIST takes it off that list; in the frequency lists, + KNOWN WORDS makes it known. Kept on the device.
- **Lexicon:** the word's panel shows the meaning to learn by default - a few words, made from the brief lexicon's gloss and (Hebrew) its entry's first sense line: בָּרָא "to create, shape, form" - as the SHORT GLOSS in LEXICON, a picker over the entry (as GRAMMAR's, kept on the device per language; no More / Less), which also picks the brief lexicon's full entry (TBESH: abridged BDB; TBESG: Abbott-Smith) or, where it has the word, a fuller lexicon: for Hebrew Brown-Driver-Briggs (also, 2026-11-03: the UBS Dictionary of Biblical Hebrew, Jastrow, Klein (etymology); for Greek the UBS Dictionary of the Greek NT (Louw-Nida) - the UBS ones marking the sense "Used here", word by word from their references: pipeline/ubs_lex.py, sefaria_lex.py; the vocab gloss a line of its own under the root word, not in LEXICON) - its full entry (all of them where BDB splits its Strong's number), its credit under it - Open Scriptures' HebrewLexicon (BDB public domain, the transcription CC BY 4.0; a work in progress, many entries an outline only), its own file downloaded the first time BDB is shown and kept. A Greek word's LEXICON picks the brief lexicon or, where each has the word, Abbott-Smith (1922, in full; public domain, the Abbott-Smith TEI project), Thayer (1889; public domain, Every Promise's CC0 edition), the Middle Liddell (Liddell and Scott's Intermediate Lexicon, 1889; Perseus, CC BY-SA - its Beta Code converted) and Dodson (public domain, CC0) - each its own file, downloaded when first shown and kept; the choice kept per language. Not found clean and openly licensed: Moulton & Milligan (the one digitization has no licence).
- **Reading aloud:** the device's voices (he-IL for the OT, el-GR for the NT): ▶ in a play bar at the bottom (the page's column: under the text, above the word's panel when it's open - nothing floats over the text) reads the chapter from the verse at the top of the view, a verse number reads that verse, each word lit as it's spoken, tapped again stops; the word's panel shows the word as written, then its dictionary form, each with a speaker that says it. Hebrew without accents (vowels kept), Greek made monotonic, Greek ὁ, εἰ, οὗ / οὐ and υἱός given in Latin letters ("oh", "e", "oo", "eeoo") (alone, the voice spelled their letters out). A verse is one utterance lit by the voice's word boundaries, or on a clock for a voice without them (never word by word unless there's a space between words). READING SPEED (0.1x - 1x) and SPACE BETWEEN WORDS (0 - 1 s; 0 reads normally, any space reads a word at a time with that pause) in ⋯, kept on the device; the voice the device's one for the language, as chosen in the device's own settings (no picker: Safari lists few voices to pages); ⋯'s "Getting other voices" says how to change it, and that Siri voices can't be used.
- **Grammar links:** each term of a word's spelled-out parsing that a grammar explains is tappable (underlined); tapped, its section takes the word's panel over (the word's info hidden) - its heading, the grammar and §, the text (the panel grows and scrolls); its ✕ closes it, the word's info back. Gesenius (Kautzsch-Cowley 1910, from Wikisource) for Hebrew; Smyth (1920, Perseus, CC BY-SA) and Machen's New Testament Greek for Beginners (1923, public domain; machengreek.com's transcription, the book's text only - its OCR slips and missing tables as they are) for Greek, every section of each in the database; the terms mapped by hand, by context (a verb's person / gender / number to the perfect's or imperfect's flexion, a suffix to the verb's, noun's or preposition's section). GRAMMAR, in the section's box once a term is tapped, picks the grammar (the section reopened in it) for the text's language (Hebrew: Gesenius - the default - or unfoldingWord's Hebrew Grammar; Greek: Machen - the default -, Smyth or unfoldingWord's Greek Grammar, CC BY-SA 4.0, still being written); another grammar in the database is another choice. Not found as clean, open text (scans or poor OCR only): Goodwin's Greek Grammar, Nunn's Elements of New Testament Greek, Davidson's Introductory Hebrew Grammar, Harper's Elements of Hebrew.
- **Notes panel (commentaries, apparatus and translations):** (2026-11-03 also: translations NJPS, Contemporary Torah, Koren, NET, Brenton, EMTV, Wycliffe; commentaries Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Ramban; apparatus HOTTP, Miqra according to the Masorah, Minchat Shai, Dead Sea Scrolls, TCENT, Sinaiticus, IGNTP, Bezae; a new ANCIENT VERSIONS group: Targums Onkelos, Pseudo-Jonathan, Jonathan - pipeline/notes_sefaria.py, notes_mss.py; SYNTAX: Sentence diagram (MACULA Hebrew and Greek, CC BY 4.0; a file per testament) - each clause a box, its phrases labeled by role with the words and glosses, clauses inside phrases nested, joining words first; the tree stored per sentence as JSON and drawn by notes.ts' drawDiagram, so the drawing changes without a rebuild: pipeline/diagram.py, its trees pinned in macula_pins.json) the icon right of ▶ opens it, in the page's column above the play bar, which stays at the screen's bottom (nothing over the text); a picker swaps the source (it and ✕ at the panel's top, scrolling away with the notes) - COMMENTARIES: unfoldingWord Translation Notes (CC BY-SA, both testaments, each note under the original words it's about), Tyndale Open Study Notes (CC BY-SA, both), Keil & Delitzsch (OT, public domain), Vincent's Word Studies (NT, public domain); APPARATUS: Editions and variants (STEPBible: TAGNT's editions - NA28/27, Tyndale House, SBL, WH, Tregelles, TR, Byzantine - and variant notes; TAHOT's Qere / Ketiv and other manuscripts' readings) and the SBLGNT apparatus (CC BY 4.0, NT); TRANSLATIONS: the verse in English - World English Bible, Berean Standard Bible, KJV, ASV, Young's Literal, Darby (public domain) and the Literal Standard Version (CC BY-SA 4.0), eBible.org's USFM, mapped to the text's verse numbers (Psalm titles verse 0; 3 John 1:15, Revelation 12:18 and the WEB's Romans 16:25-27 from where the translation numbers them). It shows the notes on the verse at the top of the view, following the scroll, or the verse being read aloud; a range note on each of its verses. Each source its own file, downloaded when first picked and kept; the choice kept per testament. Not found clean and open: Expositor's Greek Testament, Alford, Bengel.
- **Not yet:** everything else above - spoken answers, vocab for any range (a chapter, a span), more grammars (Robertson, Ewald...: see the plan), stem highlighting, following the reader's own reading aloud (recognition), search.

## Map

- `pipeline/` - Python (standard library only), run ahead of time. `fetch.py` downloads STEPBible-Data at a pinned commit into `pipeline/downloads/` (gitignored); `build_db.py` builds `public/data/cotext.db` (gitignored; its docstring lists the tables); `morph.py` turns the source's morphology codes into COTEXT's codes and spelled-out parsing (its docstring gives the scheme; its guide() the Parsing guide's rows); `grammar.py` fetches the grammars (pinned: `gesenius_revisions.json`, Smyth's hash, `machen_pins.json`), turns them into sections, and maps the parsing's terms to them (its docstring lists the tables). `notes.py` fetches the notes panel's sources (commentaries, apparatus, English translations) (pinned by URL and hash) and builds each into `public/data/notes/<id>.db`, listed in the main database's `note_sources` (its docstring lists them and the table). `bdb.py` fetches Open Scriptures' BDB (pinned by commit and hash), maps the text's Strong's keys to its entries and builds `public/data/notes/bdb.db` and the main database's `bdb` / `bdb_file` (its docstring). `greek_lex.py` does the same for the Greek lexicons - Abbott-Smith, Thayer, the Middle Liddell, Dodson (pinned by URL and hash) - into `public/data/notes/<lexicon>.db` and the main database's `greek_lex` / `greek_lex_file` (its docstring).
- `src/` - the app, TypeScript, no framework: `main.ts` (start, the heading, going to a chapter, #Gen.1 links), `db.ts` (the database in SQLite WASM, cached for offline), `reader.ts` (the chapter's interlinear), `panel.ts` (the word's panel), `grammar.ts` (the parsing's terms linked to a grammar's sections; GRAMMAR), `picker.ts` (books and chapters), `helps.ts` (the ⋯ menu: which words' helps are hidden), `study.ts` (the ☰ drawer and its screens), `paradigm.ts` (the verb forms' templates - Hebrew stems, Greek tenses and voices - their patterns, codes and meanings on "do", and the model verbs), `chart.ts` (the Verb chart), `cards.ts` (flashcards, and the Verb paradigms screen), `guide.ts` (the Parsing guide), `vocab.ts` (My vocab), `audio.ts` (reading aloud: ▶, verse numbers, the panel's 🔊 (the word), READING SPEED and SPACE BETWEEN WORDS), `notes.ts` (the notes panel: commentaries, apparatus and translations, synced to the verse).
- `public/` - copied into `dist/` as it is: `index.html`, `style.css` (the look and its color tokens), `sw.js` (the service worker), `manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/` (placeholders), `fonts/` (Ezra SIL, Gentium - OFL, each with its license), `sqlite/` (the official SQLite WASM build, 3.54.1, vendored).
- `design/` - the approved mock-ups, the reference for the look.
- `build.mjs` - the build (esbuild, the one npm dependency, pinned in `package.json` / `package-lock.json`). `serve.py` - a local server for `dist/`. `NOTICE` - the credits (STEPBible CC BY, BDB (Open Scriptures, CC BY), the Greek lexicons (Abbott-Smith, Thayer and Dodson public domain, the Middle Liddell CC BY-SA), the grammars - Smyth's CC BY-SA, Gesenius and Machen public domain -, the commentaries, apparatus and translations - three CC BY-SA -, fonts, SQLite).

## Build and run

1. `python3 pipeline/build_db.py` - downloads the data (once, about 100 MB; the grammars a few minutes more; the notes' sources about 45 MB) and builds the database and the notes' files (about 30 s).
2. `npm install` (once), then `npm run build` - into `dist/`.
3. `python3 serve.py` - http://127.0.0.1:8090/ (another port: `python3 serve.py 8095`). Ports 8000-8002 belong to Numerator's server: never use them.

## How (proposed, to confirm)

- **Phone and iPad first:** an installable web app (PWA), offline; Capacitor later for the app stores, Tauri for desktop.
- **TypeScript, minimal dependencies:** no framework (plain DOM / Web Components), one build tool (esbuild or Deno); every outside piece pinned and kept in the repo. The user was put off Node by its many libraries going out of date - keep the list to a handful.
- **Data:** a word-level database (each word: surface form, lemma, parsing, gloss, lexicon entry, position) in the official SQLite WebAssembly build, with full-text search. Built ahead of time by Python scripts from the source data; the app only downloads the result.
- **Sources (open, credit required - confirm each LICENSE file):** MACULA Greek and Hebrew (Clear Bible / Biblica, CC BY 4.0: parsing, syntax trees, glosses, semantic domains; Nestle 1904 and SBLGNT, Westminster Leningrad Codex) as the core; STEPBible Data (Tyndale House, CC BY 4.0: TAGNT's textual variants across NA / TR, TAHOT, brief lexicons); public-domain lexicons (BDB, Abbott-Smith, Dodson). Not without a licence: Nestle-Aland / UBS texts, BDAG, HALOT, ETCBC BHSA (non-commercial), the CATSS LXX morphology. A tagged Septuagint isn't openly available yet (STEPBible's TAGOT is coming).
- **Fonts:** Ezra SIL (Hebrew), Gentium Plus (Greek) - OFL, embeddable.

## Working with the user

The user often works from the iPad or iPhone via Remote Control; the Mac is the build host (its screen is broken: anything needing the Mac itself - a password, a dialog, Xcode - is marked "At the Mac:").

- **Requests:** say the expected wait at the start, work without step-by-step updates, end with one concise report.
- **Scope:** do only what's asked. Anything beyond the direct ask that takes more than about a minute is asked first, with an estimate in minutes.
- **Checking:** the one check is that it works; ask before any thorough testing.
- **Git:** commit each finished change with a short message; push only when asked.
- **Old versions:** when something is replaced, delete the old version - try things out and move on.
