# COTEXT

A Bible study app for students learning Biblical Greek and Hebrew.

## The plan (2026-11-01; first build 2026-11-02 - see Done below)

- **Core:** an interlinear (original text with English beneath each word) and parsing information for every word - each switched on and off by the reader.
- **Parsing as shown:** compact, readable without tapping, no spaces - all caps, Stem-Form-PGN with hyphens, e.g. Q-P-3MS (Qal perfect 3ms), QP-P-3MS (Qal passive), HI-W-3MS (Hiphil wayyiqtol), N-MP, a prefix joined with + (ART+N-MP, CONJ+OBJ); verbs colored by form in Hebrew (wayyiqtol, qatal, weqatal, yiqtol, volitives, participles, infinitives) and by tense in Greek (VERB COLORS in the ⋯ menu turns it off; ABOUT VERB COLORS beside it says what each color means). Converted once, when the database is built, from the source's codes.
- **A word tapped:** its concise meaning (built - see Done), its lexicon entry (STEPBible's brief lexicons, TBESH / TBESG - the same extended Strong's keys as the text) - or, as a setting, the fuller entry (BDB for Hebrew, Abbott-Smith likely for Greek; public domain) - and its parsing spelled out (Qal perfect, 3rd masculine singular).
- **Helps controls** (behind a HELPS button): a frequency slider - glosses hidden for words occurring N+ times, parsings for forms occurring N+ times, showing how many words (by frequency) you'd need to know to read them; frequency counted over the whole OT (or NT) or this book alone (book-specific vocab); HIDE KNOWN WORDS (on / off). A word's panel marks that word known by hand ("+ Known words", its gloss hidden), every form of it, on top of the slider.
- **Grammar from the word's panel** (built for Gesenius and Smyth - see Done): each part of the spelled-out parsing tappable (Qal, perfect, 3ms...) - opens that part's explanation, under the word's info, from a grammar picked in a setting (several to try, then one chosen): Hebrew - Gesenius (Kautzsch-Cowley 1910, public domain); Greek - Smyth (1920; Perseus CC BY-SA, classical), Robertson (1914, the fullest NT grammar; scans only - topics mapped to pages, text built section by section), Ewald's Elementary New Testament Greek (2022, digital text, CC BY-NC-SA - non-commercial only), Moulton, Nunn, Goodwin; linked by section or page.
- **Verb stems highlighted:** pick stems (Hebrew binyanim - Qal, Niphal, Piel...; Greek tense / voice / mood) and see them marked through the text.
- **Vocab:** flashcards for any range (a book, a chapter, any span) and by frequency.
- **Verb paradigm flashcards:** a form's pattern - the root's letters as empty dotted squares with their vowels, the form's own letters (prefixes, endings) written - and, turned over, its parsing with its meaning on "do": Q-P-3MS, "he did" (and the pattern on a model root).
- **Spoken answers:** on a vocab flashcard, say the English meaning instead of tapping - the browser's own speech recognition (Safari on iPhone / iPad included; audio goes to Apple), matched leniently against the word's glosses and lexicon senses ("create" / "created" / "to create"), showing what it heard; the reader can still mark it right or wrong.
- **Audio:** modern pronunciation for both (modern Greek, modern Israeli Hebrew - the user's choice, and what they teach): the device's built-in voices read words and verses, and reading aloud can be recognised and followed word by word.
- **Vocab:** each person's words to learn and known, as lists and flashcards (see Done).
- **Reader's version:** a selective interlinear - glosses shown only for words rarer than a frequency the reader sets; a button shows everything when unsure. A way to quiz yourself on reading.
- **Loftier goal:** Bible translation tools - translating from the interlinear into a new version.
- **Also:** generate PDFs (a print stylesheet first; Typst on the server for book-quality typesetting).

## Done (first build, 2026-11-02)

- **Data:** STEPBible-Data (TAHOT, TAGNT, TBESH, TBESG) built into one SQLite database - words, COTEXT parsing codes and their spelled-out parsing, lexicon, per-lemma frequencies (whole OT / NT and per book). MACULA's syntax trees make the sentence diagrams (notes panel).
- **Reader:** pick a book and chapter (the heading's passage on one line - the book and chapter in English and in Hebrew or Greek, each centered in its side, the Greek at the left, the Hebrew at the right; too wide, the English abbreviated, then the Greek, then both made smaller; ‹ › - at the play bar's ends, as far in as ☰; sideways its top and foot; with a mouse, the text's margins wide enough, each centered in its margin at the text's middle height - the Verse cards' and Flashcards' ‹ › too, beside the card (bars.ts' marginArrows) - step through, as does a swipe across the text: left the next, right the previous); the interlinear as in the mock-ups - original, gloss, parsing code, verbs colored by form (Hebrew) or tense (Greek); a word tapped opens the panel: dictionary form, parsing spelled out, brief lexicon entry.
- **App:** a PWA, offline after the first load (the app's files kept by the service worker; the database and the notes / lexicon files by the page: streamed into OPFS once per version and opened where they lie - SQLite in a worker - so a launch shows the text at once; without OPFS, the old way: read whole into memory from Cache Storage). The database comes as parts (2026-11-04: core 5.9 MB - books, parsings, freq, note_sources and the rest of the small tables -, lexicon 9.8, grammar 12.7, and the words by testament: ot 19.9, nt 7.1, lxx 27.1), each its own version, ATTACHed under one connection (a temporary view joins the testaments' words): an update downloads only the parts that changed, in the background - the kept older version opens at once and the new one is used from the next start; only a first start waits for its download.

## Map

- `pipeline/` - Python (standard library only), run ahead of time. `fetch.py` downloads STEPBible-Data at a pinned commit into `pipeline/downloads/` (gitignored); `build_db.py` builds `public/data/cotext.db` (gitignored; ## Build and run

1. `python3 pipeline/build_db.py` - downloads the data (once, about 100 MB; the grammars a few minutes more; the notes' sources about 45 MB) and builds the database and the notes' files (about 30 s).
2. `npm install` (once), then `npm run build` - into `dist/`.
3. `python3 serve.py` - http://127.0.0.1:8090/ (another port: `python3 serve.py 8095`). Ports 8000-8002 belong to Numerator's server: never use them.

## How (proposed, to confirm)

- **Phone and iPad first:** an installable web app (PWA), offline; Capacitor later for the app stores, Tauri for desktop.
- **TypeScript, minimal dependencies:** no framework (plain DOM / Web Components), one build tool (esbuild or Deno); every outside piece pinned and kept in the repo. The user was put off Node by its many libraries going out of date - keep the list to a handful.
- **Data:** a word-level database (each word: surface form, lemma, parsing, gloss, lexicon entry, position) in the official SQLite WebAssembly build, with full-text search. Built ahead of time by Python scripts from the source data; the app only downloads the result.

## Working with the user

The user often works from the iPad or iPhone via Remote Control; the Mac is the build host (its screen is broken: anything needing the Mac itself - a password, a dialog, Xcode - is marked "At the Mac:").

- **Requests:** say the expected wait at the start, work without step-by-step updates, end with one concise report.
- **Scope:** do only what's asked. Anything beyond the direct ask that takes more than about a minute is asked first, with an estimate in minutes.
- **A change asked for is a change everywhere:** when the user points out something to change, it's changed everywhere the same kind of thing appears, in the same change - through that kind's one rule (below), never only on the screen shown. Every like case is checked before it's reported done.
- **Checking:** the one check is that it works; ask before any thorough testing.
- **Git:** commit each finished change with a short message; push only when asked.
- **Old versions:** when something is replaced, delete the old version - try things out and move on.

## Rules for the page (public/style.css, src/*.ts)

- **One way per kind of thing.** Each kind of visual thing - a pill, an icon pill, a caption, a note, a field's label, a picker, a box, an overlay, a plain button, a list's row, a title, a bottom bar, a panel - is drawn by one rule set in style.css (its kind named in its comment). A new case of a kind goes through that rule; if it can't take the case, change the rule so it can, for every case - never a separate rule beside it for one situation. Before styling, ask "is this like anything else?"; if it is, it's drawn the same way.
- **One template, reused:** a near-duplicate is merged into its kind's rule; changing a template changes everything that uses it.
- **Tokens, never literals:** colors, text sizes, spacing, corners, lines, shadows and tints are `:root` tokens (the palette first; canvas drawings read them with getComputedStyle), used by name; an asked-for color is picked from the palette.
- **Styles in style.css, not the code:** TypeScript sets classes and the `hidden` attribute (the one way to hide anything); it writes a style only for what's measured or dynamic (a measured width, a CSS variable set from a measurement, a position).
- **One highlight:** what's picked is in the accent (`.pill[aria-pressed="true"]`, a picker's choice); what's tapped or read aloud is the accent's tint (`--tint`, `--tint-strong`).
- **Work once, use often:** what's needed per frame, scroll, tap or keystroke is worked out when what it depends on changes (a value cached, a list sorted once, a measurement kept until a resize) and read after.
